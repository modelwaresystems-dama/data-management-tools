MDA Data Pack Preparation and Readiness Methodology

Digital Zambia Acceleration Project · ZM-SZ-466193-CS-QBS · K-2 Data Governance

← Assessor consoleMDA guide

This methodology shows how to prepare a nominated dataset and its metadata so a readiness assessment can proceed without further follow-up. Two audiences use it: MDA staff who prepare and package a dataset, and the K-2 assessor who reviews what was submitted.

1.Purpose and scope

It covers a repeatable, evidence-based preparation and readiness process for representative MDA datasets and their metadata. It is limited to the K-2 Data Governance role under the Expert Services Agreement (ESA P9902): data governance and data classification services only. It is not an assessment of whole-of-MDA governance maturity, cloud readiness, legal compliance, or every data asset an institution holds.

"Ready" means enough evidence exists to run the agreed assessment. It does not mean the data has been judged accurate, lawful, optimally governed or correctly classified.

2.Assessment principles

  • Dataset-specific, not institution-wide. Each assessment concerns nominated representative datasets, not the whole estate.
  • MDA-led nomination. Institutions nominate datasets relevant to their mandate. No common dataset is prescribed.
  • Standard metadata, variable content. The headings are common across MDAs. Entities, fields, systems, volumes and rules are institution-specific.
  • Evidence readiness before findings. "Ready" means enough evidence exists to run the agreed assessment, nothing more.
  • Extracts, not live access. The assessment works from extracts and documentation, not direct access to live systems.
  • Security by substitution. If a raw sample cannot be shared, provide an approved masked, redacted, synthetic or structurally equivalent extract, plus the reason.
  • No legal determination. The pack records restrictions, retention instructions and policy references supplied by the MDA. K-2 gives no legal interpretation.
  • Proportionality. Detailed field-level evidence is required for the selected datasets, not for the whole estate.

3.Roles and responsibilities

Each nominated dataset needs named, reachable people who can explain, approve and validate it. Without them the assessment cannot stand up to challenge. Roles map to the DM Artefact Metamodel's DG roles.

4.Process overview

An MDA prepares and packages the dataset, then K-2 assesses it. Ready packs go to the CIA pilot, others loop back for remediation.

5.Step 1: Nominate datasets

Choose datasets that matter to your mandate and that you can actually explain and extract.

A good nominee is priority, representative of the mandate, explainable by available owners or custodians, and practically able to produce an approved extract or substitute. Do not nominate a nationally prescribed common dataset or a complete asset inventory.

Record each nominated dataset in the 01 Nominations schedule: official title, identifier, mandate relevance, priority, nomination rationale, and a proposed contact. The Client approves the final asset set, so nominations stay provisional until confirmed.

6.Step 2: Prepare the representative extract (D12)

Provide a safe-to-examine sample so the dataset's structure and candidate CIA implications can be reviewed.

Supply an approved extract with a field list and a README. If raw data cannot be shared, use security by substitution: provide a masked, redacted, synthetic or structurally equivalent extract, plus the reason for the restriction. An extract is never a reason to expose personal or sensitive records.

The README should state: dataset title, extraction date, period covered, record count, the fields included, any masking applied, the version, and the owner's approval. Secure transfer and document-classification instructions are set by the Client and MDA. Place the extract and its README in the 04 Representative Extract folder, and list it in 06 Evidence Index.

9.Step 5: Classification and security evidence (M5)

Record what already exists so a classification can be validated later, not assumed now.

Capture any existing sensitivity label and the confidentiality, integrity and availability considerations: what harm or service impact could follow from disclosure, alteration or unavailability. State the access, handling and sharing restrictions that apply. Use the 05 Classification tab.

The label list in the template is the Modelware default scheme; the project's Zambia-specific classification framework replaces it once confirmed at inception. Classification is confirmed in a pilot workshop. FIPS 199 is a reasoning reference for CIA impact only, not a replacement for the project framework.

11.Step 7: K-2 readiness assessment

The assessor checks the pack three ways, then records one of four determinations per dataset.

  1. Completeness. Every mandatory requirement has a response.
  2. Evidence. Tell an unsupported claim apart from documented evidence.
  3. Consistency. Compare the metadata with the schema, sample, source statement, refresh information and restrictions.
ReadyMandatory responses and essential evidence are present, consistent and safe to use.
Ready with gapsAssessment can proceed, but defined actions or evidence are outstanding.
Not readyMissing ownership, source/authority, a structure that can be read, an approved sample or stakeholder access prevents a sound assessment.
RestrictedEvidence exists but cannot yet be released under approved arrangements.

The requirements that force "not ready" if missing are D4 ownership, D5 source/authority, D7 structure, D12 representative sample, M2 technical metadata and M4 governance, plus stakeholder access for clarification. The assessor records each determination in the readiness console, which syncs to the private repository.

12.Completeness gate: self-check before you submit

Run this check before submitting so nothing needs chasing afterwards.

  • Every mandatory D and M item answered, or "not applicable" with a reason.
  • Named Data Owner, steward/custodian and technical contact with reachable details (D4 / M4).
  • Source system and authoritative status stated (D5).
  • Field list or data dictionary provided, and it matches the sample (D7 / M2).
  • Approved extract or substitute with a README and owner sign-off (D12).
  • Classification label and CIA considerations recorded (M5).
  • Every attached file listed in 06 Evidence Index with its restriction.
  • Transmittal signed with the completeness declaration.

Missing an owner, the source, a structure that can be read, or an approved sample is exactly what makes a dataset "not ready". Clearing this checklist means the pack can be assessed on the first pass.

13.Glossary and references

Key terms

  • Metadata. The descriptions that make a dataset understandable and usable: business, technical, operational, governance, quality and lineage.
  • Representative dataset. A nominated dataset that stands in for a mandate area, not the whole estate.
  • Readiness. That enough evidence exists to run the agreed assessment. It is not a judgement of data quality or compliance.
  • CIA. Confidentiality, integrity and availability. The three impacts a classification weighs.
  • Security by substitution. Supplying a masked, redacted, synthetic or structurally equivalent extract when raw data cannot be shared.
  • Lineage and provenance. Where data comes from and the main steps that shape it.

Standards that anchor the requirements

W3C DCAT 3 (dataset vs distribution; identity, contact, access rights, frequency, coverage, format, size, standards, provenance), ISO/IEC 11179-3 (metadata registry metamodel), W3C PROV-O (provenance), FIPS 199 (CIA impact, reference only), W3C DQV and ISO 8000-61 (data quality), ISO 15489 (records and retention).

The DM Artefact Metamodel (5W1H), v1.5.2, supplies the definitions, DG role list, classification labels and data-quality dimensions used in the console and the template.
