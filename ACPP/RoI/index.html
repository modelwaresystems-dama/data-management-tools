The verdict

Internal capability development outperforms external sourcing for scarce skills (Josh Bersin). Upskilling is risk mitigation for capital allocation, and in Know Your Value terms it is protected value made visible. Funded against a Value Request, the climb protects the capital, the delivery and the trust the organisation would otherwise lose, and it reports what it saved.

Dictionary of terms

Every input and calculated term used in the figures above.

Roles in scope

The data and AI roles you would build internally or source on the market.

Average loaded cost per role

The full annual cost of one role, salary plus on-costs.

Capital allocated to AI

The money committed to AI work this year that depends on skilled people to land.

Upskilling investment

What you plan to spend developing existing staff, the cost of the climb.

Cost to build one role

What it costs to take one person up the capability climb. It sets the full-build cost.

Sourcing premium 62% · PwC

The extra you pay to hire an AI or data skill on the market versus a standard role.

Project failure risk 80% · RAND

The share of AI projects that fail, mostly on skills and data-readiness gaps.

Gross exposure = sourcing penalty + failure exposure

What you carry by doing nothing. Sourcing penalty is roles × cost × premium; failure exposure is AI capital × failure risk.

Preventable share of failure 70%

The skills-related part of the failure exposure that capability can remove. The rest stays whatever you do.

Capability coverage = upskilling ÷ (roles × build cost)

How much of the capability your investment builds, capped at 100%.

Value protected = coverage × (penalty + failure × preventable share)

The exposure your upskilling removes. It covers the sourcing penalty in full and the preventable share of the failure risk.

Residual cost of inaction = gross − value protected

What is still at risk after the climb. It falls as you invest, down to the irreducible floor, never to zero.

Net benefit = value protected − upskilling

What you are ahead after paying for the climb.
