Privacy & Access Model

How “private to the learner” is achieved — and where it is enforced

The pilot rests on a simple promise that makes the Win-Win Zone work: a person’s capability results are private to that person. Managers see where the team needs to build — never a ranking of named individuals. This note explains how that privacy is enforced, and is honest about what the current demo does versus what the platform adds.

Be clear in the room — the interactive demo models the privacy boundary; it does not yet enforce it. A browser-only prototype holds all its data on the client, so whoever has the page has the data. Enforcement is a platform capability (Phase 0: authentication, tenant isolation, learner identity, consent), delivered before any real learner data is entered. Present the boundary as the governance model, enforced by sign-in and role-based access in the platform — not as a guarantee the demo itself provides.

How privacy is enforced in the platform

1
Identity & authentication.
Every learner signs in. The system knows whose request each action is, so access can be decided per person rather than assumed.
2
Authorization — the real control.
Server-side rules decide what each identity may read. A learner’s session can retrieve only their own capability record; a manager’s session can run only aggregate queries. Individual rows never leave the server for a manager, so there is no “switch to another person” to find.
3
Tenant isolation.
DLK’s data is fenced from every other tenant (DLK = tenant #1). One organisation can never read another’s learners or evidence.
4
Small-number suppression (k-anonymity).
Aggregate figures are withheld when a group is too small to be non-identifying — “1 of 1 meets target” would out the person. Manager views show a result only when enough people sit behind it.
5
Consent & lawful basis.
Consent is captured before data is collected, under a lawful basis (POPIA / GDPR). The learner is told what is held, why, and for how long; evidence has a defined retention and expiry.
6
Controller / processor split.
DLK is the controller of the Article 4 Assurance Record; Modelware is the processor, acting only on DLK’s instructions under a Data Processing Agreement.
7
Auditability.
Access is logged — who read or changed what, and when — so the privacy boundary is not just asserted but evidenced.

Demo today vs. platform build

Now — the prototype

  • Privacy is a design boundary and UI convention.
  • All cohort data is on the client (browser / repo).
  • The Manager view shows aggregates because it is coded to — not because it is prevented from seeing individuals.
  • Purpose: prove the model and the value, fast.

Platform — enforced

  • Privacy is enforced server-side by auth + authorization.
  • Data lives behind the server; clients receive only what their identity is allowed.
  • Managers are technically unable to retrieve an individual’s result.
  • Tenant isolation, suppression, consent and audit in force before real data is entered.

Governance roles

The learner owns their result and their evidence. They see their full capability picture and personal roadmap; nobody else sees it as theirs.
DLK (controller) decides the purpose and policy for the data, and owns the Assurance Record.
Modelware (processor) runs the service on DLK’s instructions under a DPA, and does not use individual results for its own purposes.
Why this matters for the Win-Win Zone — people share honest evidence only when they trust it will not be used against them. Enforced privacy for the individual, with aggregates for the organisation, is what lets the pilot produce real capability evidence rather than defensive self-reporting.
