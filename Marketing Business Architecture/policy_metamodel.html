Policy Meta-Model

The reusable structure lifted from the KSU NDMO/NDI policy workbooks (Retention & Disposal, Backup & Recovery, Storage, Monitoring, Access – Staff, Access – Patient). Every one of the ~31 policies is the same object model, so a single meta-model lets the Policy Inspector link Controls, SOPs, Evidence and the rest — exactly as the glossary links Terms. This page is for inspection and sign-off of that structure.

Governance chain: Policy → Principles · Themes/Articles · Management Intent → Controls → implemented by SOPs that create & deliver Evidence → approved & checked against Acceptance Criteria, measured by KPIs → audited by CCAA / PDRE.

The Control is the atom — a rich object, not a one-liner

This is the point where a policy stops being prose and becomes governable. In these documents each policy carries 38–40 controls across 7–8 themes (~5 per theme); each control has twelve elements plus a Responsible and an Accountable role, and fans out to its own statement, evidence, acceptance rule, KPI and SOP. Modelling a policy as a single control is what makes it unconvincing — the depth lives here.

How a control is implemented: a control is a required state, not an action. The SOP is the repeatable procedure that implements the control — it creates and delivers the evidence (its outputs), the accountable Owner approves that evidence, and the Acceptance Criterion passes only when the approved evidence is present and verifiable. Control → implemented by SOP → produces Evidence → approved → satisfies Acceptance.

Two layers most policy models miss

Traceability viewsassociative

Not entities but the joins that prove completeness: Principle_Control_Map (every principle embodied by a control, with a constraint type), Theme_Control (every control in exactly one theme/article), and CCAA_Traceability (every control back to its regulatory anchor, principle and management intent). These are what let the Inspector answer “why does this control exist?”

Assurance of the documentCCAA · PDRE

CCAA checks regulatory & principle coverage and raises SME questions where a source may be missing. PDRE is a defect log of the policy document itself — findings, exact changes, verification and status. Together they make the policy auditable, not just authored.
