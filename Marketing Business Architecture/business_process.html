Traceability spine — outcome to decision

Where this process sits in the business-centered spine: the Business Outcome it serves — through KPI, value stream and capability — to the Business Decisions it makes, and on to the data, AI use-cases and controls. Every process traces to a named outcome and an accountable decision.

Process definition — architecture gate

The process defined against the Process Architecture gate: trigger and flow, the decision it changes, where AI acts, the human-oversight pattern, the records and controls, and how performance improves.

Process structure

The eight structural elements of this business process, as specified: primary actor and supporting actors, the information it requires, its main and alternate flows, the business rules that govern it, the output it produces, and the future system requirement.

Decisions this process makes — DMN, inputs & knowledge

Each governed decision the process reaches, with its input data (the information and critical data elements it consumes), its knowledge sources (glossary, semantic model, policy and rules it applies), its rule set, decision owner and advising agent — and a link to the full DMN decision-requirements diagram and decision table.

Process Step Architecture

Each atomic step and what it is accountable for — execution type, decision, information-concept CRUD/lifecycle, data-product impact, control, record and evidence. Every step carries one primary execution type on the H / A / AIS / AIE / DS taxonomy.

Lifecycle — BPMN 2.0 swimlane

The same steps as a governed flow — one lane per department / actor that performs the human work, an AI / Automation lane for everything the system / AI performs (Automated, AI-Supported, AI-Executed and Data-Support steps), and an AI Steward lane where each human-in-the-loop decision is reviewed by its accountable role before the flow hands back, and an Exception & alternate flows lane for the boundary events. Decisions render as business-rule tasks with an XOR gateway, and every outcome ends in a message event (create / notify) — all inside a lane.

Framework alignment. Processes are classified on the APQC Process Classification Framework (category → process group → process → activity → task) and, where they touch banking service domains, mapped to BIAN service domains. This execution-level layer adds the org-specific step grain beneath those reference spines — every step carrying one primary execution type (H / A / AIS / AIE / DS) with an explicit Responsible and Accountable party.

Information Concepts & lifecycle

The business concepts the processes create, read, update and retire — each owned/stewarded by a capability, realised by a data product, and governed through an explicit state lifecycle.

Record & Evidence architecture

Records are evidence that a step occurred, a decision was made, a control operated or an obligation was fulfilled — each with an owner, retention and disposal rule, and a linked evidence pattern.
