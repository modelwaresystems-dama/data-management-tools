Load a Data State Transition model

This viewer ships without any model in it. Drop an .fts.json file here, or choose one from your private model store. Nothing is uploaded anywhere; the file is read in your browser only.

Expected shape: meta, rules, globalStates, subStates, activities, transitions, guards, entry and exit conditions, invariants, KA interactions, governance chain, relationships.
Why a state model

The same lifecycle, two ways of describing it

On the left, the lifecycle as a chain of boxes. On the right, what the same stretch of lifecycle looks like once each stage is a state with a contract: conditions to enter, conditions to remain, permitted activities, conditions to leave, and guarded, authorised transitions. Every number on the right is read from the loaded model.

      States, sub-states and transitions

      Each large container is a Global State. Inside it, the sub-states in sequence, with nested states indented. Click any state to open its contract below. Dashed purple arrows are recursive or iterative transitions: the lifecycle going back on itself, which a linear diagram cannot show.

      sub-state transition recursive / iterative global transition (guarded, authorised)
      Select a state above to see its contract: entry conditions, invariants, permissible activities, exit conditions, transitions, artefacts, evidence, roles and decision rights.
      Transitions

      Every permitted movement, and what it takes

      A transition is eligible only when the source exit conditions, the transition guards and the target entry conditions all hold. Eligibility is still not authorisation: a material transition also needs the applicable decision right. Click a row to open its contract.

      Knowledge Areas

      Orthogonal state machines that constrain the lifecycle

      Knowledge Areas are not stages and not sub-states. Each has its own state model, and it contributes predicates, guards, artefacts and evidence to the global transitions. A Data Asset therefore occupies a Global State and several KA states at once: the Composite Data Management State.
