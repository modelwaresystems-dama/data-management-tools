1 · No duplicate terms across glossaries

Checks whether the same term (or an alias of one) is defined as a canonical term in more than one glossary — the kind of split-brain definition that undermines a single source of truth.

2 · No term sitting independently

An orphan is a term whose definition references no other defined term, and which no other definition references back — it floats free of the model. These are the terms to either connect or retire.

3 · Missing terms

Concepts that definitions lean on but that are not themselves defined anywhere in the estate. Ranked by how many definitions depend on them — the top of this list is your highest-leverage backlog. Click a referencing term to inspect it.

Relationships · associative entities

Where the taxonomy says what a thing is, the relationship layer says how things connect. The Group Membership associative entity resolves the many-to-many between a Party Group and its member Parties, and carries the liability the group itself cannot bear.

5 · Taxonomies

Two taxonomies emerge from the data. The is-a hierarchy is built from each term’s curated Hypernym (is-a) column — the single designated parent; parents shown in amber are referenced as classes but not yet defined (good candidates to formalise). The subject-area facets are the curated groupings each glossary already carries.

Taxonomy tree

Two views. Curated model is the corrected Actor / Party-Role design. A Party is now one of three clean kinds — Natural Person, Legal Entity (a registered corporation or statutory body), or Party Group (an informal/temporary collective such as a Stokvel, consortium, household or joint venture). Bank, Government Body, Regulator and Transport Provider live in the Organisation Role branch (roles a Legal Entity plays); group membership and liability flow through Group Role. Curated is-a is the hierarchy from each term’s Hypernym column. Amber nodes are classes not yet defined; click a defined term to inspect it in the graph.
