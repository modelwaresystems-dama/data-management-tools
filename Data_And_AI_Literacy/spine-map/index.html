Inside a Knowledge Area — the context diagram

A Knowledge Area is not a flat node. It contains a context diagram that is central to the whole ontology. It decomposes into activity phases (Planning · Development · Operations · Control); each phase holds best practices (activities); each best practice requires a Deliverable — an output or artifact. The Deliverable is the Evidence: whether the Learner can produce what's required, and how well — scored by three instruments. The full DAMA pattern: a Definition and Goals & Principles frame the area; Suppliers provide Inputs; Participants perform the work and Consumers use the deliverables; Tools, Techniques and Metrics support and measure it. Mastery is assessed per deliverable.

Best practice → Deliverable. Every activity in a phase requires a specific output — the Deliverable (artifact).
Deliverable = Evidence. Evidence is whether the Learner can produce the required Deliverable — and how well.
Three assessment instruments. A produced Deliverable is scored for Quality (Quality Scorecard), Value delivered (Value Scorecard — useful change, KYV) and Stakeholder Feedback (satisfaction).
Mastery per Deliverable. Acquired / Required / Desired are set at the Deliverable level, then roll up to the Knowledge Area.

Framework ↔ fit — activity affinity

The DMBOK framework defines what work a Knowledge Area needs — activities, organised by phase. Thinking Styles and Strengths define who is naturally suited to that work. They meet at activity affinity: each phase's activities favour certain thinking styles and strengths. This is the link from the capability/framework side to the person/fit side — and it is fit, never Mastery.
