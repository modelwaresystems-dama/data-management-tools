Metadata & Dataset Requirements, Guide for MDAs

Digital Zambia Acceleration Project · ZM-SZ-466193-CS-QBS · what to supply for each nominated dataset

Who this is for. Officers in each Ministry, Department or Agency (MDA) who will prepare the evidence pack for a nominated dataset. You do not need to be a data specialist, this guide explains each item in plain language.

What we're asking. For each dataset you nominate, we ask for a consistent set of descriptions (its "metadata") plus a safe sample. The headings are the same for every MDA; the content is specific to your institution. We assess whether enough evidence exists to proceed, not whether your data is perfect, lawful or optimally governed.

A few principles. You nominate datasets that matter to your mandate (we don't prescribe a common dataset). We work from extracts and documentation, not live-system access. If you can't share a raw sample, send an approved masked, redacted or synthetic substitute and tell us why. We don't make legal or classification determinations, we record what you supply.

Part A, About the dataset (D1-D13)

D1Dataset identityRequired
What to send usThe dataset's official name, any other names it's known by, a short plain description, and an identifier if it has one.
What this coversA stable name and identifier that let everyone refer to the same dataset without confusion.
What "good" looks likeStable name/ID and understandable description supplied
Examplee.g. "TPIN Taxpayer Register (CIT)", ID MOFNP-ZRA-TPIN-001, aliases "Taxpayer Master".
D2Purpose and mandate useRequired
What to send usA few sentences on what the dataset is for, which service or process uses it, and who the main users are.
What this coversWhy the dataset exists: the business purpose, the service or process it supports, and who relies on it. This is business metadata, not a legal statement of mandate.
What "good" looks likePurpose and supported process are explicit
Examplee.g. "Supports VAT assessment and refund processing; principal consumers: ZRA Domestic Taxes, MoFNP revenue forecasting."
D3Nomination rationaleRequired
What to send usWhy you chose this dataset for the pilot, how important it is to your mandate and what makes it a good example.
What this coversThe MDA's stated reason this dataset is a good pilot candidate, its priority, criticality to the mandate, and how it adds diversity to the pilot. The final asset set is approved by the Client.
What "good" looks likeMDA explains relevance to mandate and pilot diversity
Examplee.g. "High-priority, core to revenue mandate, representative of large transactional administrative data."
D4Ownership and contactsRequired
What to send usNames and contact details for the Data Owner, the person who looks after it day-to-day (steward/custodian), and a technical contact.
What this coversThe named, reachable people accountable for the dataset, Data Owner, Steward, Custodian, technical contact. Without these, no one can explain, approve or validate the data, so assessment cannot be defended.
What "good" looks likeNamed organisational roles and reachable contacts
Examplee.g. Data Owner: Director Domestic Taxes; Business Data Steward: J. Mwale; Technical contact: DBA team.
D5Source and authoritativenessRequired
What to send usWhere the data comes from (which system), who owns that system, and whether this is the original record or a copy/derived version.
What this coversWhere the data comes from and whether this is the authoritative system of record, a copy, or a derived product. The assessment team records what the MDA states; it does not certify authority.
What "good" looks likeSource and authority status are stated and internally consistent
Examplee.g. "Authoritative system of record, TaxOnline (ZRA); not a derived extract."
D6Subject/entity coverageRequired
What to send usWhat the data is about, the main things, people or events it records, and the fields that identify each one.
What this coversThe real-world subjects, entities and events the dataset represents, and the key fields/business keys that identify them.
What "good" looks likeScope and key fields can be understood
Examplee.g. Subjects: taxpayers (natural persons + legal entities); events: returns, assessments; business key: TPIN.
D7Structure and schemaRequired
What to send usA list of the fields with, for each, its name, meaning, type, and whether it is a key (a data dictionary or annotated field list).
What this coversEnough structural metadata to interpret the data without reverse-engineering it: field names, meanings, datatypes, keys and relationships.
What "good" looks likeAt minimum: field name, meaning, datatype and key indicators
Examplee.g. Annotated field list: TPIN (char10, PK), TaxpayerName (varchar), RegDate (date)...
D8Representation and volumeRequired
What to send usThe file format and roughly how big it is, number of rows/records and size.
What this coversThe physical representation and rough scale: format, encoding, approximate row/file counts and size. Estimates are acceptable if marked as such.
What "good" looks likeFormat and approximate scale documented
Examplee.g. CSV, UTF-8, ~4.2M rows, ~1.1 GB, partitioned by tax year.
D9Coverage and historyRequired, "not applicable" accepted if you explain why
What to send usThe time period it covers, how far back it goes, the areas/organisations included, and anything deliberately left out.
What this coversThe temporal, geographic and organisational boundaries of the data and its granularity, plus any material exclusions. Spatial coverage may legitimately be N/A.
What "good" looks likeBoundaries and material exclusions stated
Examplee.g. 2015-2025, national, individual-taxpayer grain; excludes pre-2015 archived records.
D10OriginationRequired
What to send usHow the data is collected or produced, for example captured at application, from a survey, or derived from another system.
What this coversHow information enters the dataset, administrative capture, transaction, survey, derivation or acquisition, and the major upstream input.
What "good" looks likeOriginating activity and major upstream input identified
Examplee.g. "Captured at e-filing submission; upstream input: taxpayer self-service portal."
D11Refresh and timelinessRequired
What to send usHow often it is updated, when it was last updated, and any expected delay between an event and it appearing in the data.
What this coversHow current the data is and when it changes: refresh frequency, last refresh date, expected latency and version. Distinguish how often data is collected from how often it is published.
What "good" looks likeFrequency, currency date and known delays documented
Examplee.g. "Refreshed nightly; last refresh 2026-10-23; published monthly."
D12Representative sampleRequired
What to send usA safe sample extract with a short README, or, if raw data can't be shared, a masked/redacted/synthetic version plus the reason, approved by the owner.
What this coversA safe-to-examine extract, or an approved redacted/masked/synthetic/structurally-equivalent substitute plus the reason for restriction, so structure and candidate CIA implications can be examined. Security-by-substitution: if a raw sample can't be supplied, a substitute must be.
What "good" looks likeUsable extract/substitute, period, record count, masking and approval stated
Examplee.g. "Masked 1,000-row extract, 2025 data, TPIN hashed, approved by Data Owner 2026-10-20; README attached."
D13Sharing and dependency contextRequired, "not applicable" accepted if you explain why
What to send usWho you receive this data from and who you send it to, by what method (file, API), how often, and any agreements involved.
What this coversThe material data exchanges around the dataset, who provides to it and who receives from it, via which interfaces/APIs, and under what agreements. Direction of dependency matters; no interface engineering is assessed.
What "good" looks likeMaterial exchanges and dependency direction documented
Examplee.g. "Receives NRC data from Dept of National Registration; exports monthly aggregates to ZamStats via SFTP."

Part B, About the metadata (M1-M10)

M1Business metadataRequired
What to send usA clear, plain-language description of the dataset that a non-technical colleague would understand.
What this coversThe business-facing description: a clear, non-circular definition with purpose, subjects, keywords and consumers, such that a knowledgeable user understands the dataset. Headings are common across MDAs; values are institution-specific.
What "good" looks likeClear, non-circular description with business context
M2Technical metadataRequired
What to send usThe technical details needed to read the data: schema, field types, keys, format, encoding and any constraints.
What this coversThe technical description needed to interpret the extract directly, schema, fields, datatypes, keys, relationships, format, encoding, constraints, and it must align materially with the sample.
What "good" looks likeSchema and sample align materially
M3Operational metadataRequired
What to send usIts current operational state: source, update schedule, last update, size, and version/status.
What this coversThe operational state of the data: source, refresh schedule, last refresh, volume, availability window and version. Documented unknowns are acceptable. This is not an infrastructure-readiness assessment.
What "good" looks likeCurrent-state values or documented unknowns supplied
M4Governance / stewardshipRequired
What to send usWho is accountable for the dataset, owner, steward, custodian, approver, and how to reach them with an issue.
What this coversIdentifiable accountability for the dataset: owner, custodian, steward, approver and issue contact. A contact route must exist. No enterprise-wide RACI is assessed.
What "good" looks likeRoles and contact route present
M5Classification / securityRequired
What to send usAny existing sensitivity label, notes on the harm if it were disclosed/altered/unavailable, and access/handling/sharing rules.
What this coversExisting security labelling and the confidentiality/integrity/availability considerations, plus access/handling/sharing restrictions, enough to run a facilitated CIA review.
What "good" looks likeExisting labels and evidence available for facilitated CIA review
M6Data-quality metadataRequired
What to send usAnything you already know about data quality, known issues, existing checks or rules, thresholds, and any quality reports.
What this coversWhat is already known about fitness for use: known issues, affected fields, existing rules, measures, thresholds and accepted exceptions. 'Unavailable' is a valid documented answer. Readiness records what is known; it does not assert quality is satisfactory.
What "good" looks likeIssues/rules documented or explicitly stated as unavailable
M7Lifecycle / retentionRequired
What to send usThe retention or disposal rule that applies (or a reference to the schedule) and the next review date, or 'not determined'.
What this coversThe lifecycle instruction currently applied: creation/status, retention instruction or schedule reference, archival/disposal status and review date. 'Not determined' is a valid recorded answer. The assessment team does not determine legal retention.
What "good" looks likeExisting requirement/reference, or "not determined", is recorded
M8Reference / master / identifierRequired, "not applicable" accepted if you explain why
What to send usAny code lists, classifications or identifier schemes the data uses, and where those come from, with versions.
What this coversThe controlled values that make the data interpretable and joinable: code lists, classifications, identifier schemes and master-data sources with versions. 'None' is accepted only with an explanation.
What "good" looks likeApplicable standards and owners/sources identified
M9Interoperability / exchangeRequired, "not applicable" accepted if you explain why
What to send usDetails of any data exchanges: interface name, who it is with, format, mapping, the standard used, and frequency.
What this coversThe metadata needed to interpret exchanged data consistently: interface name, provider/consumer, format, mappings, standard/profile and frequency. No low-level integration design is assessed.
What "good" looks likeMaterial exchange metadata is present
M10Provenance / lineageRequired
What to send usA high-level picture of where the data originates and the main steps it goes through to reach its current form.
What this coversHigh-level source-to-dataset lineage that is intelligible: originating activity, upstream datasets, major transformations and derived outputs. Mandatory at source level; field-level detail is optional supporting evidence.
What "good" looks likeHigh-level source-to-dataset lineage is intelligible

How your submission is assessed

ReadyMandatory responses and essential evidence are present, consistent and safe to use.
Ready with gapsAssessment can proceed, but defined actions/evidence are outstanding.
Not readyMissing ownership, source/authority, interpretable structure, approved sample/substitute or stakeholder access prevents defensible assessment.
RestrictedEvidence exists but cannot yet be released under approved arrangements.

"Ready" never means your data has been judged accurate or compliant, only that there is enough evidence for the agreed assessment to go ahead. Missing an owner, the source, an interpretable structure, or an approved sample is what makes a dataset "not ready".

What this assessment does not do

So expectations are clear, the readiness assessment does not:

  • judge how mature your whole institution's data governance is;
  • inventory or classify every dataset you hold;
  • decide legal compliance, lawful basis or statutory retention;
  • assess cloud, migration, infrastructure or engineering readiness;
  • choose platforms or design interfaces;
  • buy or install any metadata or classification tools;
  • certify that your data quality is good;
  • make the final classification decision for you (your authorised officers do that);
  • ask every institution for the same dataset; or
  • take over management of your project.
